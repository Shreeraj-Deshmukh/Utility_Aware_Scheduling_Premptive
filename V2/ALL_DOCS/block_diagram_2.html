USRT V2  ·  architecture at system level  ·  11 Sep 2026

Four Lanes, One Spine

Every solver in this codebase answers the same question — map each job Ti,j ↦ ⟨Px, fz, k⟩ — against the same two scarce resources. What differs between the lanes is only which of those three decisions is made by search and which by an optimiser. This page is the map at that level; the functions come after.

The instanceusrt/utils.py · usrt/models.py

What actually enters the system

Every lane — exact, heuristic, online — begins from one three-element tuple returned by a test-case module, and immediately derives the same handful of tables from it. Nothing downstream reads the raw file again.

Loaded — load_testcase()

  • processors — id + frequency list
  • tasks — e_m, e_o_k[], p_i, u_i
  • B_BUDGET — scalar energy cap

Derived once — utils.py

  • h = lcm(periods), the hyper-period
  • quantum = gcd(periods)
  • cum[i][k] — cumulative work at f_max
  • N_job[i] = h / p_i
  • job_r, job_d — release & deadline

Returned — solver contract

  • seg_k[(i,j)] — segment level k
  • freq_idx[(i,j)] — frequency index z
  • utility — the objective Φ
  • energy — total E, checked ≤ B

Three modules make up the substrate every lane shares. models.py is the single source of truth for the energy model — E(w,f) = α·w/f + β·f²·w with α=0.15, β=1.0, so energy per unit work g(f) bottoms out at f* ≈ 0.42 and running below f_max is genuinely cheaper. dbf/ holds the timing law: window_slack, min_slack_for_job, check_all_timing. output.py does the printing. Only heuristic v1 departs, carrying its own incremental state in state.py.

Three decisions, two constraints

Hold this diagram in your head and the rest of the codebase falls into place. The processor choice touches only time; frequency and segment count touch both resources — and frequency is the only decision that can convert one into the other.

Lane 1 · exactILP.py · usrt/solvers/ilp_v2·v3·v4.py

The ILP lane: four solvers, one model

ILP v1 lets Gurobi choose the processor too. The other three fix the mapping first and hand an identical Phase 2 to the solver — so v2, v3 and v4 differ from each other in nothing but where their mapping came from.

SolverFilePhase 1 — mappingPhase 2
ILP v1ILP.pynone — Gurobi picks xjoint, paper §III
ILP v2usrt/solvers/ilp_v2.pyquantum SPS on e_mbyte-for-byte identical
paper §IV
ILP v3usrt/solvers/ilp_v3.pyquantum SPS on predicted work — mapping/predictive.py estimates what optional work the budget affords, then backs off 1.0 → 0.5 → 0.25 → plain SPS if DBF fails
ILP v4usrt/solvers/ilp_v4.pyheuristic v6's search verbatim — SPS + budget refine (1b′) + local search (1c), scored by v6's own pipeline

Why v3 and v4 exist. SPS packs by mandatory work; Phase 2 then spends the budget on optional work. The packer is balancing a quantity the finished schedule does not carry. v3 attacks that in the packer (predict the optional work first); v4 sidesteps it by borrowing the mapping search that measurably beat the plain ILP — heuristic v6 outscored ilp_v2 on mean utility over 900 instances, which can only have been the mapping.

Lane 3 · onlineusrt/online/ · usrt/solvers/online_demo.py

The online lane consumes a schedule, not an instance

This is the structural fact that makes the online phase confusing until you see it. It does not solve the problem. It takes a finished offline schedule as its input and improves it at runtime, when jobs finish sooner than their worst case said they would.

FileRole
online/value_function.pythe DP's representation — a Pareto frontier of (time, energy) requirements rather than a dense grid, so float costs need no quantisation

online/dp.pybuilds one table per processor, right-to-left over its EDF chain; every suffix answered by one precompute

Lane 4 · the harnessusrt/gen/ · usrt/runner/ · make_figures.py

Two registry gaps worth knowing before you go looking. ILP v1 lives at ILP.py in the repo root, outside the package — it is reachable through run_models.py but is not in run.py's solver list. ILP v4 has a full adapter and dispatch entry but is likewise missing from run.py's SOLVERS dict, so python run.py tc.py ilp_v4 will not find it. The online lane has no runner adapter at all, which is why it has no sweep-scale evidence.

Reading orderwhen you go function-level

Where to start next

The dependency order below is also the comprehension order — each step only needs the ones above it, so nothing forward-references.

  1. The substratemodels.py, then utils.py, then dbf/slack.py and dbf/check.py. Roughly 200 lines total, and every other file assumes them.
  2. One complete simple pipelineheuristic_v4.py end to end — quantum SPS plus phases 2 to 6, no mapping refinement to distract you. It is also run.py's default.
  3. The mappermapping/quantum.py, then dps_sps.py and ps.py beneath it. This is the single most consequential stage in the codebase.
  4. The two clever phasesphases/greedy.py (its three cases) and phases/freq_trade.py (the shadow prices λ and μ). Everything else is bookkeeping.
  5. The mapping variantsrefine_mapping.py → budget_refine.py → multistart.py, in that order, which is also v5b → v6 → v7.
  6. The exact laneilp_v2.py — short, and reading it after the heuristics shows you exactly which decisions the heuristic was approximating.
  7. The online lanevalue_function.py first — the representation is the hard part — then actions.py, dp.py, controller.py. Run worked_examples.py to see the DP reproduce the design document's tables.
