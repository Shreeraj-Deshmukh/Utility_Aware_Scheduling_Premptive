Fixed 19 Sep usrt/online/{actions,dp,controller}.py

ONLINE-FREQ — the online phase revised the frequency of a job that had already started

Paper §II: once a job starts executing it cannot execute with a different frequency. The online DP chain is ordered by EDF ((deadline, release, task, job)), and on_completion re-planned every job at chain position > c, writing a new freq_idx. Under non-preemptive execution “later in the chain” implies “not yet started”; under preemptive EDF it does not — a long-period job can start, be preempted by a shorter-period release, and still sit downstream in deadline order, so it became eligible for revision after it had already run.

The fix, and why it needed no clock. The register posed this as a trade: track start times (correct, but the online phase carries no notion of “now”) versus restrict the chain to r ≥ t_event (cheap, but conservative and lossy). It is not a trade. On one processor with no migration, the completing job c is ready and higher-EDF-priority for the whole of [r_c, finish_c), so a later-deadline job d cannot hold the CPU anywhere in that window; the only way d ran before c finished is in the gap before c was released. Hence d has provably not started ⇔ job_r[d] ≥ job_r[c] — a comparison of two static release times. Exact, not conservative.

Two gates now scope each event's DP build: a job released before the completer has its frequency frozen (k may still grow — only the frequency write was ever unsound), and a job whose own completion has already fired is excluded outright (it is resolved history; you cannot retroactively lengthen a job whose actual (dt, de) is already banked). The second gate also resolves the long-noted mismatch between the simulator's release-ordered events and the DP's deadline-ordered chain.

Evidence. Reproducer clean in 4 of 4 configurations (was: violation in 3 of 4). Worked examples still reproduce every design-doc table cell-for-cell. The gate is load-bearing — 931 of 1862 build_job_actions calls take the excluded path on testcase.py — but an A/B against the ungated behaviour returns identical numbers (online added 30.2829 either way). Stated precisely: the admissibility hole was real and is closed; on this instance it was never exercised in a way that moved a committed number. That cannot be generalised to the sweep without running it.
Fixed 19 Sep usrt/online/value_function.py · prune() / compose()

DP-PRUNE — the online DP did not terminate on a realistic instance

Not previously filed here. It surfaced only because ONLINE-FREQ prompted the first end-to-end run of the online phase on a real test case rather than a toy one.

compose() built the full |actions| × |downstream frontier| cross-product and then ran prune() — an all-pairs O(n²) scan — over the whole thing, checking it against max_frontier only afterwards. The cap that exists to bound this never got the chance. On testcase.py (8 frequency levels, up to 6 optional segments) that is ≈14,336 candidates per level ⇒ ≈2×108 comparisons per level, ≈28 levels per processor, on every build — and a build happens at construction and once per event.

Evidence. run.py testcase.py online never completed — killed at 180 s having printed nothing past Offline schedule built, i.e. still inside the first OnlineController.__init__, before event one. Confirmed pre-existing, not introduced by the ONLINE-FREQ work: git stash and re-run on the untouched tree hangs at the identical point. This is AGG-STATES / Part-II I6 landing for real, after being filed as low-severity and unverified.
Fix. prune() is now an exact O(n log n) Pareto sweep: visiting in (req_t asc, req_e asc, value desc) order makes the req_t half of the domination test automatic, leaving a prefix-maximum query over rank-compressed req_e, served by a Fenwick tree. Aggregating before pruning would have been a 3-line alternative but would bucket candidates even when the exact frontier would have fitted, so it was rejected — the sweep returns the same frontier, trading nothing. Never completes → 7.4 s. value_function.py is imported only inside usrt/online/, so no offline solver is affected.
Re-measured 30 Sep — keep usrt/phases/freq_trade.py

6B-INERT — Phase 6b fires rarely, not never

The original finding was underpowered, not wrong-headed. “0 moves in 72 instances” and “0 in 20” were read as inertness. At the rate since measured, zero in 72 has probability 0.98472 ≈ 31% and zero in 20 ≈ 72% — together ~23% likely even with 6b behaving exactly as it does. Those samples could not detect a 1-in-60 event.

Paired A/B, 608 comparisons (30 Sep) — identical instances, 6b swapped for a no-op, nothing else changed; tight-energy grid ρ = 0.25–0.5:

solverinstances improvedruntime overhead
heuristic_v5b2 / 125 (1.6%)+5.2%
heuristic_v74 / 125 (3.2%)+11.1%
heuristic_v62 / 125 (1.6%)+5.9%
heuristic_claudeoptimal1 / 125 (0.8%)+3.1%
heuristic_v5b, full ρ = 0.2–1.01 / 108 (0.9%)+0.5%

0 regressions in all 608, as the best-so-far restore guarantees. Wins run +0.9% to +6.9% utility and land entirely at ρ = 0.4–0.5 — so “it matters once energy binds” is directionally right, just rare.

Why it is rare. Measured exit points over 54 instances: 74% exit with λ = 0 before evaluating a single candidate, 19% find no feasible profitable downshift, only 7% ever run a round. The root cause is structural and was not among the three originally listed: 6b's entry condition is exactly what Phase 5 is built to destroy — λ > 0 needs a segment that is time-feasible but energy-blocked, and Phase 5's case ii.B has the identical predicate and clears those with multi-donor stacking immediately beforehand. The three original causes (global μ for a per-processor resource; one-downshift atomicity; break on the first non-improving round) are all real but secondary — each only operates inside a round.

Decision (30 Sep): keep as-is, correct the documentation. It never loses, it wins several percent precisely where the heuristic is weakest against the ILP, and the runtime cost is modest. Deleting it would trade that tail for ~5% runtime. The freq_trade.py docstring no longer claims to close a 40–58% gap — that figure predated the spec.py rewrite and was never re-measured.
Fixed 1 Oct usrt/phases/swap.py

SWAP-BREAK — Phase 6 aborted the whole search on one failed candidate

When the best candidate failed check_all_timing at EXECUTE, the else-branch reverted both jobs and ran break — ending the entire local search, discarding every remaining ranked candidate from that scan and all later iterations.

The path is unreachable by construction. Adding a segment to the receiver raises DBF demand in exactly the windows containing its [r, d] interval, and min_slack_for_job scans exactly that set — measured with the giver already tentatively removed, i.e. in the very state that holds at EXECUTE. The giver only removes work, which can only lower demand. The pre-check is therefore exact, not conservative. Measured: 0 reverts in 19 committed swaps over 54 instances.

Why it was written that way. The old log line read "cross-window timing violation" — the author believed the pre-check covered only some windows, so a failure meant "state I don't understand, stop". The safety net is reasonable; the break is the wrong reflex, and the belief behind it is false.

Fix (option C). break → blacklist the pair and continue. The blacklist is not optional — the search is deterministic and the revert restores the exact prior state, so a bare continue would re-select the same pair forever; it is cleared after each successful swap since the fill pass moves seg_k. A new _first_violation() reports where (check_all_timing returns only a bool). It then raises AssertionError after the search completes — raising rather than logging because adapters._suppress() redirects stdout at the fd level during sweeps, so a log line is invisible exactly where it matters, while an exception surfaces as a status=error row; raising after the loop means no utility is sacrificed to the diagnostic.
Verification. Paired before/after over 288 rows (4 solvers × 72 instances): byte-identical on status, feasibility, utility, energy and error — total utility 17194.396288 both sides, 0 error rows. Plus fault injection, since the path never runs naturally: forcing 1 and 3 bogus failures confirms it terminates (no infinite loop — the real risk of break→continue), keeps searching, and raises with usable diagnostics; the 0-injection control raises nothing.

V5AB-COMPARE — the variant ladder is not a controlled comparison

Phase 6b is applied to 4 of 7 heuristic variants: present in v5b, v6, v7, claudeoptimal; absent from v3, v4, v5a.

Evidence. 12 Sep 2026, direct grep of phase_freq_utility_trade across all seven solvers.
Consequence. v5a vs v5b is read as “Refine 2a vs 2b”, but it is really “2a without 6b vs 2b with 6b”. The “one capability per step” reading of the ladder no longer holds. Update 30 Sep: this entry used to hedge that the confound might be nominal because 6b contributed nothing measurable. That hedge is falsified — the paired A/B above shows 6b improving 1.6% of v5b instances and 3.2% of v7's, by up to +6.9%, concentrated at ρ = 0.4–0.5. The confound is real, and it bites in exactly the tight-energy regime the ladder is most often read in.

C8-HARMONIC — the hand-written testcase is not harmonic

Periods are 10, 20, 20, 40, 60, 120. The paper assumes a harmonic chain, and 40 ∤ 60, so this instance sits outside the stated model. Still true on 12 Sep.

The generator is correct — usrt/gen/spec.py builds periods as base × 2^m, so every generated set is harmonic. Only the legacy hand-written testcase.py violates it. Since that file is the default for run.py and appears throughout the docs, any single-instance figure quoted from it is off-model. Cheapest fix: change 60 to 80.

INT-PERIOD — int(t['p_i']) silently truncates non-integer periods

Periods are coerced with int() in 25 places. A period of 10.7 becomes 10 with no warning, changing the hyper-period, the job count and every deadline. Harmless while all generators emit integers; a silent corruption if that ever changes.

4. Examined, not defects

Phase 5 sorts by u_i, paper §V.5 says “utility density” GREEDY-ORDER u_i is the correct key.
