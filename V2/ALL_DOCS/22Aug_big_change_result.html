Three algorithmic changes letting the heuristics trade frequency for optional segments, plus one latent bug they exposed. Re-measured on the same 90 test cases as the 21 August dry run, so every number below is directly comparable.

2 · What changed

Change 1 — Phase 5 case ii.B: global, multi-donor energy donation

Case ii.B ("lower another job's frequency to fund this segment") already existed but carried three restrictions, two of them unjustified:

RestrictionVerdict
Donor had to be on the same processor Wrong — energy is a global budget; a job on P1 can fund a segment on P0.
Donor had to have lower utility rate (u_i2 ≤ u_is) Wrong — downshifting costs the donor zero utility (frequency does not appear in the utility function, paper Eq. 8). The rule guarded a cost that does not exist.
Single donor, single levelCapped how much energy could ever be raised.

Donors are now drawn from all processors, ranked by time paid per unit energy freed, and several may contribute until the segment becomes affordable. Guarded so it only fires when energy (not timing) is the blocker, skips any donor at or below f*, and reverts every donor if the final DBF check fails. (phases/greedy.py)

Change 2 — Phase 4 ordering by efficiency

Phase 4 previously scaled frequencies down in task-index order, which can burn DBF slack on poor trades and then fail to reach the budget on instances that are actually schedulable. Candidates are now visited by energy saved per unit time cost, descending — the cheapest route to feasibility first. (phases/aggressive.py)

Change 3 — new Phase 6b: shadow-price frequency↔segment trade

The principled version of the trade, with no tuned constants. Two resources are scarce — energy (global) and time (per-processor DBF slack) — and frequency is the exchange rate. For a segment of work w on task i at frequency f the marginal rates are conveniently independent of w:

utility per ENERGY = u_i / g(f)        g(f) = alpha/f + beta*f^2
utility per TIME   = u_i * f

Shadow prices are read off the current solution each round:

lambda = max u_i/g(f)  over segments blocked ONLY by energy   ("value of one more unit of energy")
mu     = max u_i*f     over segments blocked ONLY by time     ("value of one more unit of time")

accept a downshift  iff  lambda * dE  >  mu * dT

Then Phase 5 is re-run to spend the freed energy. The phase keeps a best-so-far snapshot and restores it unless utility strictly improves, so it can never return a worse schedule than it was given — worst case is a no-op. It never crosses f* (requires dE > 0), and every move is DBF-verified. Wired into heuristic_v5b (after Phase 6) and heuristic_claudeoptimal (before Phase 7). (phases/freq_trade.py)

Bug found — Phase 8 restored segments but not frequencies

_phase_iterated_greedy (claudeoptimal Phase 8) snapshotted and rolled back seg_k but not freq_idx — even though the Phase 5 and Phase 6 calls inside its reconstruct step both mutate frequencies. A rejected iteration therefore left an inconsistent (seg_k, freq_idx) pair whose energy could exceed the budget.

The bug was latent: the old case ii.B was so restricted that frequencies barely moved. Change 1 made ii.B fire often, and claudeoptimal's feasibility immediately dropped 84 → 79. Snapshotting and restoring freq_idx alongside seg_k returned it to 84. This is a pre-existing defect that the new work exposed, not one it introduced.

6 · greedy_SPS_Baseline — the naive floor

A deliberately minimal reference point: Quantum SPS mapping only (no Refine Mapping, no swap, no iterated greedy), every job pinned at f_max with no DVFS in either direction, and optional segments added greedily in decreasing utility density until timing or energy blocks them. (solvers/greedy_sps_baseline.py)

The baseline solves 50/90 — exactly the score the real heuristics had before the Phase 3 fix. That is not a coincidence: with frequency frozen at f_max, the baseline is infeasible precisely when the mandatory workload alone busts the budget, which is the identical condition the old Phase 3 test was applying. It independently confirms that the pre-fix heuristics were behaving as though they had no DVFS at all.

Mean optimality gap where it produces a schedule: 51.7%, versus 16.1% for v5b and 16.1% for claudeoptimal.

What the baseline isolates. At ρ=1.0 the baseline is essentially optimal (0.6% gap, identical to claudeoptimal) — when energy is abundant, f_max genuinely is the right choice and none of the machinery matters. The entire value of the staged heuristics — and of DVFS — is concentrated in exactly the region where the budget binds.

8 · What is left

  1. The residual gap is concentrated where both resources bind at once. At ρ≥0.8 or α≤0.4 the heuristics are now within roughly 5–12% of the exact optimum. The worst remaining points are ρ=0.4 (v5b 31.4%) and α=0.7 (v5b 38.8%) — instances that are simultaneously energy-starved and timing-tight, where a single-level greedy exchange cannot find the joint assignment the ILP solves directly.
  2. Phase 6b currently moves one level at a time. A multi-level or multi-job move (the frequency analogue of Phase 7's double-giver) is the obvious next step for those corners.
  3. claudeoptimal still dominates v5b at nearly every point, and now matches the exact solver on feasibility. It remains the better default than v5b for tight budgets, for both the runner and the online phase's offline baseline.
  4. Positive knock-on for the online phase. Offline schedules now commit mid-range frequencies instead of pinning f_max, which is exactly the precondition the online audit (15 Aug, Finding A / Recommendation 1) identified as missing: online Option 4 regains upward headroom and Option 3 gains a live customer. Worth re-running the online phase to see whether DVFS now activates at loose budgets too.

9 · Heuristic v6 — budget-aware mapping

The 22 August work above improved how the pipeline uses a mapping. v6 attacks the mapping itself, which the ILP-v2 decomposition showed was the dominant error source at tight budgets (44 points at ρ=0.4, versus 14 from the frequency/segment search).

What v6 changes

Only the mapping stage; phases 2–6b are v5b's, unchanged.

StageIdea
1b′ budget-aware refine The objective flips on a parameter-free regime test (E_mand_fmax ≤ B): time-bound → balance utility density (as before); energy-bound → concentrate it. Measured against optimal ILP mappings, the optimum concentrates at ρ=0.4 (spread 0.504→0.730, 3/4 instances) and balances at ρ=1.0 (0.472→0.295) — the old "always minimise max UD" rule was backwards at tight budgets. Concentration is measured by Σ UD(x)², which rises exactly when mass concentrates.
f̂ operating point DBF and the load-balance guard are evaluated at the frequency the budget actually forces, f̂ = max{f : E(W_mand, f) ≤ B}, not at f_max. A tight budget inflates effective times by 1/f̂, so a mapping "feasible at f_max" can leave a core with almost no slack where it matters. (Predicted f̂=0.6 on the ρ=0.4 instances; the optimal solver used 0.6 on essentially every job.)
1c mapping local search Best-improvement over single-job reassignments, each candidate scored by the utility the full pipeline actually achieves — the real objective, not a proxy.
guard 1b′ optimises a proxy, so it can land on a mapping the pipeline scores lower than plain SPS. Both are scored on the real objective and the better is kept, so the refinement cannot hurt.

A pure heuristic beating SPS+exact-ILP is only possible because v6 improves the mapping, which ILP v2 is structurally stuck with. It is optimal on 25/50 instances (median gap 0.15%) and matches the exact solver on feasibility at 84/90.

10 · Tie-breaking — results that no longer depend on dict order

A sort whose key can tie, with no secondary key, silently resolves the tie by list order — which here traces back to Python dict insertion order. Several such sorts sat in the live path, and one of them was swinging results by 22%.

The symptom. Re-running heuristic_v5b on testcase.py with the same processor assignment but the jobs listed in a different order produced 227.33 / 227.33 / 205.16 / 177.28 for as-is / EDF / reverse / shuffle. heuristic_v4 — which does not run Refine Mapping — was rock stable at 222.24, which localised the fault immediately.

Why DBF is immune but the refinement was not

window_slack selects the jobs contained in a window and sums their effective times. A sum is commutative, so DBF feasibility cannot depend on order — and under preemptive EDF the runtime order is chosen by deadline anyway, not by us. What was order-sensitive were the greedy search orders, above all the pair ordering inside Refine Mapping:

pairs.sort(key=lambda p: abs(task_ud[p[0][0]] - task_ud[p[1][0]]))

Every pair drawn from the same two tasks has an identical ud-difference, so ties are the common case, not the exception.

The root cause took two attempts to find. Adding a tie-break alone did not fix it — and the first attempt made the default case worse (227.33 → 177.28). The reason: itertools.combinations() preserves input order, so the same unordered pair appears as (a,b) or (b,a) depending on dict insertion order, and that leaked straight back through the new tie-break. The actual fix was sorted(mapping.keys()) before combinations(), in both refine_mapping and budget_refine.

What was changed, and on what grounds

SiteTie-break addedRationale
refine_mapping._build_doublets most-imbalanced processor pair first Meaningful — a swap between two already-balanced processors is motion without progress; the imbalanced pair is where max UD can actually fall.
quantum.py deferralheaviest job first among equal deadlines Meaningful — when capacity forces a deferral, defer whatever frees the most.
controller.py commitcheaper decision first among equal gains Meaningful — the DBF trim drops what no longer fits, so spending scarce resources on the cheaper of two equally valuable options leaves room for more.
left_shift.pyEDF, then earliest release Meaningful — the earlier-released job is available sooner, so packing it first wastes less idle time. (Diagnostic only; it had been reporting 2.0 vs 12.0 slack for the same input.)
dps_sps.pytotal load, then position Determinism only — no principled preference exists among equal gaps, and none was invented.
