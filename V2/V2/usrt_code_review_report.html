A. Fundamental Anomaly — Energy Constants Invert the DVFS Premise

Critical usrt/models.py:10-11   ILP.py:51-52

The chosen α = 1.0, β = 0.5 make fmax the energy-optimal frequency — so frequency scaling can never save energy

The paper's energy model (Eq. 2) is implemented faithfully:

E(i, k, z) = α · (Σei,q / fz) + β · fz² · Σei,q   ⟹   energy per unit work: g(f) = α/f + β·f²

The minimum of g(f) is at the critical frequency f* = (α / 2β)⅓. With α = 1.0, β = 0.5:   f* = (1.0 / 1.0)⅓ = 1.0 = fmax exactly. For every f < 1, g′(f) < 0 — energy strictly decreases as frequency increases.

Verified numerically: 
f=0.3: energy per unit work = 3.3783
f=0.5: energy per unit work = 2.1250
f=0.7: energy per unit work = 1.6736
f=0.9: energy per unit work = 1.5161
f=1.0: energy per unit work = 1.5000   ← global minimum
critical frequency (α/2β)^(1/3) = 1.0

Cascading consequences — large parts of the published algorithm are dead code:

Paper elementCode locationStatus under α=1, β=0.5
Heuristic step 4 — "reduce freq first … until timing/energy constraints are satisfied" usrt/phases/aggressive.py (Phase 4) Structural no-op. The code itself prints "No reductions (α=1.0, β=0.5 → min energy at f_max → no-op)".
Ch1 — "time slack available but energy not → decrease frequency of J*" Phase 5 logic, all solvers Never beneficial — decreasing frequency costs energy, never frees it.
Ch2 — "(time slack avail, energy budget not) incrementally dec freq of others" usrt/phases/greedy.py:89-125 (Case ii.B) Dead code: esaved is always ≤ 0, so the continue on line 103 always fires.
Online options 3 & 4 (Section VI) — trade Δt ↔ Δe via frequency (not implemented — see C.9) Would be meaningless under these constants anyway.

The net effect: the entire problem degenerates into binary segment selection (knapsack) at fmax under timing (per-processor DBF) and one global energy cap. usrt/solvers/heuristic_claudeoptimal.py:10-14 states this explicitly as its "key insight." This means the v1–v5 experiments never exercise the frequency-assignment dimension that distinguishes USRT from plain reward-based scheduling.

Fix: choose constants whose critical frequency lies inside the frequency range, e.g. α = 0.15, β = 1.0 ⟹ f* = (0.15/2)⅓ ≈ 0.42 — then DVFS decisions become real trade-offs and Phase 4 / Case ii.B / Ch2 paths come alive. Re-run all solver comparisons afterwards; the v1–v5 ranking will likely change. If the constants are intentional, the paper's Section V narrative (freq-decrease cases) must be rewritten to match what is actually evaluated.

B. Genuine Bugs

Critical usrt/state.py:185-206   usrt/phases/aggressive.py:55-72

B1. ScheduleState.try_dec_freq has no energy guard — heuristic_v1 can violate the energy budget (constraint C3)

try_dec_freq checks timing only, then unconditionally executes self.slack_energy -= delta_e. Under the default constants, decreasing frequency increases energy (delta_e > 0), so energy slack silently goes negative.

# usrt/state.py — try_dec_freq (abridged)
delta_e  = _energy(cum_ik, new_fz) - _energy(cum_ik, old_fz)   # > 0 under default α,β
# timing windows are checked here …
# … but there is NO check:  if delta_e > self.slack_energy: return False
self.freq_idx[(i, j)] = z_cur - 1
self.slack_energy -= delta_e        # can go negative → budget B violated

It is invoked in a loop by aggressive_freq_scaling_state (used by heuristic_v1): while state.try_dec_freq(i, j): … — which drives every job toward fmin subject only to timing, wasting energy and potentially exceeding budget B before Phase 3 even starts.

Contrast with the dict-based variant usrt/phases/aggressive.py:43-44, which correctly requires E_new < E_cur before applying a reduction.

Fix: in try_dec_freq, add both (1) an energy-improvement test (delta_e < 0) or at minimum (2) a budget guard if delta_e > self.slack_energy + 1e-9: return False — mirroring the guard that try_inc_seg/try_inc_freq already have.
Critical usrt/mapping/quantum.py:71-99, 127-131

B2. Quantum SPS capacity accounting never releases completed jobs — Phase 1 degenerates for most of the hyper-period

proc_util[px] += e_m / p_i is accumulated for every job ever assigned and is never decremented when a job's deadline passes. A task therefore contributes N_job × (e_m/p_i) to the running total instead of its true utilization e_m/p_i. Capacity (1.0 − proc_util[x]) is exhausted early and every later quantum appears overloaded.

Confirmed by live run (python run.py testcase.py heuristic_v5b): 
  [   0,  10]   util=0.674
  [  10,  20]   util=0.919
  [  20,  30]   OVER(util=1.164)
  [  30,  40]   MAND_OVERUTIL      ← every remaining quantum reports this
  …
  [ 110, 120]   MAND_OVERUTIL

  [!] 22 leftover/unmapped job(s). Assigning to min-util processor.   ← 22 of 30 jobs bypass SPS
  DBF: FEASIBLE ✓   ← schedule rescued only by the repair pass

  Processor utilisation after SPS:
    P0: 15 jobs  util=3.1495  ← OVER 1.0     ← same broken metric; actual EDF schedule is feasible
    P1: 15 jobs  util=3.3469  ← OVER 1.0

So on this testcase, "Phase 1: Quantum SPS" is effectively SPS for the first ~25% of the hyper-period, then a greedy min-util fallback plus DBF repair. Any experimental claim about the quality of the SPS mapping is not being measured. The util=3.25 ← OVER 1.0 diagnostics printed by v5a/v5b solvers use the same flawed cumulative metric and falsely alarm on feasible schedules.

Fix: make capacity time-windowed: subtract a job's e_m/p_i from proc_util at the quantum where its deadline passes (event-based release), or evaluate utilization per quantum interval over the jobs whose windows overlap it, instead of a monotone cumulative sum. Update the post-SPS diagnostic to print true task-level utilization (Σ over tasks, not jobs).
High usrt/phases/greedy_state.py:50-64

B3. heuristic_v1 Phase 3 can strip a high-utility segment to admit a low-utility one — and never rolls back failed freeing moves

Move (B) calls try_dec_seg on any overlapping job, with no utility-density comparison. The paper's Ch3 explicitly restricts segment removal to "jobs with lower utility density". Because job_order visits high-u jobs first, by the time a low-u job j* is processed, overlapping high-u jobs are fair game for stripping — net utility can decrease.

# (B) decrement segment of an overlapping job — no u_i guard:
for (ii, jj) in overlapping:
    if state.seg_k[(ii, jj)] > 0:
        if state.try_dec_seg(ii, jj):   # may remove a HIGHER-utility segment
            freed = True
            break
…
if not state.try_inc_seg(i, j):
    break                               # freeing move NOT rolled back → utility lost for nothing
Fix: (1) guard move (B) with u_other · Δe_other < u_star · Δe_star (compare marginal utilities, not just u_i); (2) snapshot the freeing move and revert it if the follow-up try_inc_seg fails.
High usrt/phases/greedy_leftshift.py:141-173

B4. heuristic_v2's "last resort" step conflates paper cases Ch2 and Ch3 and has no net-utility check

Step 3 simultaneously decrements the other job's frequency and removes one of its segments (z_o_new = z−1 and k_o_new = k−1 in one move). The paper treats these as separate cases: Ch2 = adjust frequency of others (utility preserved), Ch3 = remove segments of lower utility-density jobs. The only guard here is u_other < u_star, which ignores segment sizes:

Example: remove a 3.0-unit segment of a u=2.0 task (−6.0 utility) to add a 0.5-unit segment of a u=2.1 task (+1.05 utility) ⟹ net −4.95 — accepted by the current guard.

It is also gated by a magic constant E_SLACK_THRESHOLD = 1.0 with no justification or scaling to the instance.

Fix: split into two moves matching Ch2/Ch3; accept only if u_star·e_seg_star − u_other·e_seg_other > 0 (net marginal utility positive); derive the slack threshold from instance scale (e.g., fraction of B) or remove the gate.
Medium usrt/phases/swap.py:119-126

B5. Phase 6 swap aborts the entire search when one candidate fails the timing re-check

If the best (giver → receiver) swap fails the full check_all_timing, the loop executes break — terminating the whole local search instead of blacklisting that pair and trying the next-best candidate. Remaining improving swaps are silently abandoned.

Note the deeper inconsistency: min_slack_for_job evaluates exactly the windows affected by adding execution time to the receiver, so the revert path should be unreachable. If it ever fires, it signals a state-consistency bug elsewhere — which makes silently breaking doubly wrong.

Fix: maintain a set of rejected (giver, receiver) pairs for the current iteration and continue the search; log loudly (or assert) when the "unreachable" revert triggers.
Medium usrt/output.py:18   run.py

B6. Every solver crashes on a default Windows console (cp1252) before doing any work

UnicodeEncodeError: 'charmap' codec can't encode character 'α' in position 24

print_instance_summary emits α, β, ←, ─ and box-drawing characters. On Windows with the default cp1252 console encoding, the program dies at the banner. Reproduced during this review; the workaround was PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8.

Fix: add at the top of run.py: sys.stdout.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8", errors="replace") (Python ≥ 3.7), or replace the special characters with ASCII (alpha=, beta=, ->).

C. Paper ↔ Implementation ↔ Notes Contradictions

Critical Key_Notes&Deductions.txt vs usrt/dbf/* & paper §II

C7. Preemption model conflict — DBF is not a sufficient test if optional segments are atomic

Your notes state: "Preemption is NOT allowed mid-segment. Optional segments are atomic blocks — limited preemption windows only." The paper states: Preemptive EDF. Every feasibility check in the codebase (check_all_timing, check_dbf_proc, ILP constraint C2, ScheduleState windows) is the classical fully-preemptive EDF demand-bound test.

Under limited preemption (segment-atomic execution), the DBF condition is necessary but not sufficient: a lower-priority job's non-preemptable block can push a higher-priority job past its deadline even when all window demands fit. As written, every solver (and the ILP) can certify schedules that are infeasible under the segment-atomic model in the notes.

Decide which model is real, then: if fully preemptive — fix the notes; if segment-atomic — augment the test with a blocking term (DBF(t1,t2) + max non-preemptable block of jobs with deadline > t2 ≤ t2−t1, i.e., the non-preemptive/limited-preemptive EDF analysis), and re-validate all reported results.
High testcase.py vs paper §II (Task Model)

C8. Testcase violates the paper's harmonic-period assumption

The paper assumes harmonic periods (each period divides every larger one). The testcase uses periods {10, 20, 20, 40, 60, 120}: 40 ∤ 60, so the set is not harmonic. The code survives via h = lcm = 120, but any analytical claim that leans on harmonicity (nesting of DBF windows, quantum classification, online DP bounds) is not actually covered by this instance.

Fix: either make the testcase harmonic (e.g., replace p=60 with p=80 or p=40·2k), or formally relax the assumption in the paper (the implementation already handles general periods through the LCM).
High usrt/ (entire package) vs paper §VI

C9. Section VI (Online phase) is entirely unimplemented

A grep across usrt/ finds no code for early completion, time/energy slack harvesting, the DP-based (Δt, Δe) distribution, or the two-early-completions approximation. If the online algorithm is part of the paper's contribution list, this is the largest open gap: there is nothing to evaluate it with.

Fix / plan: implement as a discrete-event simulator over the offline schedule: sample actual execution times < WCET, detect early completion, and apply the right-to-left DP from §VI. The offline DBF window structures in usrt/dbf/ can be reused for the bound computation the paper asks for ("better bounds must be computed from schedule").
Medium usrt/solvers/heuristic_v5a.py:98-102 (same in v5b, v3, v4)

C10. Infeasible-mandatory early exit gives up too early for general (α, β)

Phase 3 declares the instance infeasible if mandatory-at-fmax exceeds B. That is only valid when fmax is energy-minimal (the current, degenerate constants). With corrected constants (critical frequency < 1), mandatory segments at a lower frequency are cheaper — the instance may be perfectly feasible.

Fix: before declaring infeasibility, scale mandatory segments to their energy-minimal feasible frequency (subject to DBF), then test the budget. This also matches the paper's step ordering (step 4 reduces frequency precisely to create energy slack).

D. Edge Cases & Hygiene

Medium

D11. Floating-point drift in incrementally-maintained slack

usrt/phases/greedy.py / swap.py maintain E_slack by hundreds of incremental ±= updates, each accepted with a 1e-9 tolerance; usrt/state.py does the same for DBF window slack with no re-synchronisation. Accumulated drift can both leak budget and spuriously reject moves.

Fix: recompute E_slack (and optionally DBF) exactly from scratch once per outer pass — cheap, and bounds drift to one pass.
Low

D12. int(t['p_i']) silently truncates non-integer periods

usrt/utils.py:67 and several other places cast periods with int(). A period of 10.5 silently becomes 10, corrupting release times, deadlines, the hyper-period, and the quantum.

Fix: assert float(t['p_i']).is_integer() at load time (in load_testcase), or support rational periods explicitly.
Medium

D13. Repair can return PARTIAL and the v5 solvers never check

usrt/mapping/repair.py:60 caps repair at 100 iterations and returns (mapping, ok); quantum_sps_mapping prints "PARTIAL" but still returns the broken mapping, and v5a/v5b proceed to optimise optional segments on top of an infeasible mandatory mapping without ever re-checking.

Fix: propagate the feasibility flag out of quantum_sps_mapping and hard-stop (or fall back to fewer optionals / report infeasible) in the solvers when repair fails.
Low

D14. The "left-shift" diagnostic raises false alarms

Phase 2 simulates non-preemptive EDF while the model is preemptive. On the live run it printed "Jobs with negative left-shift slack: 6, min −17.67" for a schedule that is provably DBF-feasible. A reader of the log would conclude the schedule is broken.

Fix: label the output explicitly as a non-preemptive lower-bound proxy ("informational, not a feasibility verdict"), or compute slack from the DBF windows instead.
Medium

D15. heuristic_v1's single-pass SPS is window-blind and unrepaired

usrt/mapping/single_pass.py balances e_m/p_i over the whole hyper-period with no awareness of when jobs execute and runs no DBF check or repair. Jobs sharing the same activation window can pile onto one processor while the balance metric looks perfect; infeasibility is only reported (not fixed) in the final printout (DBF feasible : False).

Fix: run check_dbf_mandatory + repair_mapping after the single-pass mapping, exactly as the quantum variant does.

E. Suggested Improvements

  1. Fix the energy constants and re-benchmark everything (ties to A). With f* inside the frequency range, Phase 4, Case ii.B and the Ch2 paths activate, and the relative ranking of v1–v5/claudeoptimal will likely change. Today's comparisons only measure segment-knapsack quality.
  2. Unify on the incremental ScheduleState for Phases 5–6. check_all_timing is recomputed from scratch inside triple-nested loops — O(passes × jobs² × windows) — while an incremental DBF structure already exists in usrt/state.py but is only used by heuristic_v1. This is the main scalability ceiling for larger task sets.
  3. Generalise Phase 6 beyond 1-for-1 swaps. claudeoptimal's double-giver is a step; a cleaner formulation is ratio-greedy knapsack — rank segments by utility per unit of the binding resource (slack of the tightest DBF window, or energy) with regret-based repair, since under the current constants the problem is a multi-dimensional knapsack.
  4. Cut the doublet cost in Refine Mapping. usrt/mapping/refine_mapping.py builds and sorts all O(n²) job pairs per call. Restrict doublets to (max-UD processor × others) and keep processor UDs in a heap; same result, much cheaper on large instances.
  5. Prune DBF windows. All (t1, t2) ∈ Ax×Dx pairs are enumerated. Standard pruning — skip windows wider than the busy-period bound and dominated windows — typically reduces constraint counts 5–10×, which also directly shrinks the ILP v2 model.
  6. Add an independent final validator. A separate module (no shared state with the solvers) that re-checks DBF + energy from scratch on whatever any solver returns. It would have caught B1 and B3 immediately, and gives every experiment a trustworthy pass/fail stamp.

F. What Was Verified as Correct

✓ ILP v2 (usrt/solvers/ilp_v2.py) is a faithful transcription of paper §IV: C1 (one (k,z) per job, Eq. 11), C2 (per-processor DBF over Ax×Dx windows, Eqs. 12–13), C3 (global energy ≤ B), and the objective matches Eq. 8 (ui × optional execution at fmax).
✓ Energy / effective-time formulas (usrt/models.py) match paper Eqs. 1–2 exactly (the issue in §A is the constants, not the formula).
✓ Utility model — u_i · (cum[i][k] − cum[i][0]) matches Eq. 8's ei,q-at-fmax convention (utility independent of executed frequency).
✓ DBF window enumeration (t1 ∈ arrivals, t2 ∈ deadlines, jobs fully contained) is the standard processor-demand criterion — correct for fully preemptive EDF (see C7 for the caveat).
✓ Refine Mapping 2a/2b (usrt/mapping/refine_mapping.py) matches §V.A Steps 1/2a/2b. Two deliberate, documented deviations are sensible: the mandatory-utilisation guard, and the strict max-UD-decrease rule in 2b (anti-cycling). Verified live: UD spread 1.4522 → 0.1236 in 6 swaps with DBF preserved.
✓ Phase 5 Case (i) shortcut — using min_slack_for_job ≥ add_time without a full DBF re-check is provably sufficient (adding demand to a job only affects windows containing it), and the windows enumerated are exactly those.
